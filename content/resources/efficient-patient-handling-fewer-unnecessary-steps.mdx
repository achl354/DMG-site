## Efficiency means fewer steps, not more speed

Efficiency in patient handling is not about asking staff to move faster.
It is about designing the workflow so fewer unnecessary actions are
required.

Every additional handling step consumes time and may increase physical
exposure for both staff and patients.

## Where unnecessary steps occur

A transfer or repositioning workflow may become inefficient when staff
need to:

- locate equipment in another area
- move the patient onto an intermediate device
- change equipment between departments
- repeat a movement because alignment was not maintained
- wait for a compatible accessory
- manually adjust the patient after transfer
- return equipment through a complex processing pathway

Individually, these delays may appear minor. Across a high-volume
service, they can become significant.

## Map the current process

A practical workflow review can begin by documenting:

1. where the patient starts
2. where they need to go
3. each movement and adjustment performed
4. the staff and equipment involved
5. delays or workarounds
6. what happens to the equipment after use

This can identify steps that do not directly contribute to patient care.

## Select equipment that simplifies the pathway

Appropriate equipment may help:

- reduce the number of handling stages
- support movement and positioning within one workflow
- improve compatibility between clinical surfaces
- reduce repeated manual adjustment
- simplify setup and storage

However, adding more equipment does not automatically improve efficiency.
Each solution should have a clear purpose and integrate with the
surrounding process.

The objective is a safer, more predictable workflow, not simply a larger
equipment inventory.
