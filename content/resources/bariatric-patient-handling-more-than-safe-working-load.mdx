## Safe working load is not the whole story

Safe working load is an essential consideration when selecting equipment
for bariatric-capable care.

However, a high weight rating alone does not create a complete bariatric
patient-handling system. The equipment must also suit the patient's body
dimensions, mobility, clinical condition and the environment in which care
is delivered.

## Look beyond the number

Two patients of the same weight may require very different handling
plans.

Factors to consider include:

- body shape and weight distribution
- ability to assist with movement
- limb size and positioning
- skin integrity
- respiratory considerations
- pain and clinical condition
- available space
- the intended transfer or repositioning task

Equipment must provide suitable dimensions and support, not only
sufficient load capacity.

## Review the full pathway

Bariatric-capable planning should address more than one transfer device.

Facilities may need defined solutions for:

- lateral transfer
- repositioning and bed boosting
- controlled turning
- floor recovery
- hoist transfer
- seated transfer
- transport
- emergency or evacuation situations

The patient may move through several departments during an episode of
care. Each area should be able to accommodate the patient safely and
respectfully.

## Protect dignity and consistency

Bariatric care should avoid improvised solutions and unnecessary delays
while appropriate equipment is sourced.

A planned approach can support:

- greater privacy and dignity
- clearer staff responsibilities
- better equipment availability
- fewer last-minute workarounds
- more consistent handling processes

Bariatric-capable care is therefore not defined by the highest safe
working load in the equipment catalogue. It requires a coordinated system
that considers the patient, task, environment and complete care pathway.
