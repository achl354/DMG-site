## There is no single universal solution

There is no single product that is appropriate for every patient-handling
activity.

Moving a patient laterally between two surfaces is fundamentally different
from turning them in bed, recovering them from the floor or transferring
them using a hoist. Each workflow involves different movements, forces,
clinical objectives and environmental constraints.

## Begin with the task, not the product

Before selecting equipment, define what needs to happen:

- Is the patient being transferred, repositioned, turned or elevated?
- Will they remain on the same surface?
- Can the patient participate in the movement?
- Is the activity planned or unplanned?
- What space is available around the patient?
- Are there imaging or procedural requirements?
- What infection-prevention pathway applies?
- How frequently will the equipment be required?

A product that works well for one task may add unnecessary steps or create
new limitations in another.

## Different workflows need different solutions

A connected patient-handling system may include solutions for:

### Lateral transfer

Moving a patient between a bed, trolley, imaging table or procedural
surface while minimising physical effort and friction.

### Repositioning and bed boosting

Moving a patient within the bed to improve alignment, comfort or clinical
access.

### Controlled turning

Supporting side-lying, hygiene care, wound access and pressure-care
workflows.

### Floor or seated recovery

Raising a patient from the floor or a low position using an appropriate
recovery process.

### Hoist transfer

Selecting a sling and hoist configuration that is appropriate for the
patient, attachment type and transfer objective.

### Seated transfer

Supporting patients who retain sufficient mobility to participate in a
surface-to-surface movement.

## Design a system, not a collection of products

Effective patient handling depends on more than owning multiple devices.
Each product must have a defined role, be available where the relevant
task occurs and be supported by appropriate training and procedures.

The goal is not to find one universal device. It is to build a system in
which the right solution is matched to the right movement.
