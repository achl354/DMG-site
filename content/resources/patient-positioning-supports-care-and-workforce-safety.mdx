## More than a staff-safety issue

Patient positioning is sometimes discussed primarily as a staff-safety
issue. It also plays an important role in the patient's care experience.

Appropriate positioning can support comfort, clinical access, body
alignment and the delivery of hygiene, wound and pressure-care activities.

## The connection between positioning and care

A patient may need to be repositioned to:

- improve comfort
- support a clinical procedure or assessment
- provide hygiene or wound access
- change pressure exposure
- maintain body alignment
- assist with respiratory or mobility-related care

The quality of the movement matters as much as the final position.

A rushed or poorly controlled repositioning task may result in the patient
being left misaligned, uncomfortable or requiring further adjustment. This
creates additional handling for staff and further disruption for the
patient.

## Reduce unnecessary adjustment

Patient-handling equipment should help staff reach the intended position
with fewer repeated movements.

This may involve:

- reducing friction during movement
- maintaining the patient's alignment
- supporting controlled side-lying
- improving access to the relevant body area
- allowing staff to complete the task from a safer working position

The goal is not simply to move the patient. It is to place them
appropriately for the care activity that follows.

## Consider the complete positioning pathway

A positioning workflow should account for:

1. the starting position
2. the movement required
3. the equipment and staff needed
4. the intended clinical position
5. the duration the patient will remain there
6. how the patient will be returned or repositioned again

Viewing positioning as a complete clinical pathway helps align patient
comfort, care requirements and workforce safety.
